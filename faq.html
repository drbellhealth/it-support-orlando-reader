Orlando Managed IT Services FAQ

Common questions Orlando-area businesses ask before engaging a managed IT services provider — answered plainly.

You're new to outsourced IT support. What is it?

Short version: you pay a fixed monthly fee per person on your staff. In exchange, your team calls one number when they have any IT problem — frozen Outlook, printer chaos, password lockout, application crash, Wi-Fi flakiness, the works. A technician picks up, fixes it, moves on. No more cobbling together vendors or leaning on the 'office IT person' who isn't actually an IT person.

How much should you expect to pay?

Around $90 to $175 per user per month for solid end-user IT support in Orlando. The range depends on what's bundled — response-time SLA, after-hours coverage, on-site dispatch radius. A 20-person company is probably $2K-$3.5K per month.

Will you have to sign a contract?

Depends. Some Orlando providers make you commit to two or three years. Dytech Group runs month-to-month. Always ask about the termination clause before signing. Office line: (407) 678-8300.

Are the help desk people in the US?

Some yes, some no. Dytech's help desk is US-based. Bigger national providers sometimes use offshore tier-one. Ask. It matters when you're trying to explain something fiddly at 4pm on a Tuesday and you don't have time for the back-and-forth.

What happens the first time someone calls?

Pretty simple. Your staffer dials the help desk number, gives their name and company, describes the problem. A technician picks up the ticket, often starts a remote session right there, fixes the issue. Ticket gets logged and closed. The whole thing usually takes minutes for routine stuff. After a couple weeks your team stops thinking about it and just calls the number when something's wrong.

What if your office has tricky printers and a wonky Wi-Fi setup?

First week of onboarding usually surfaces all of it. The discovery phase inventories everything. Wonky printers, weird network configuration, applications nobody documented, equipment from three different vendors — it all gets mapped. Then the support team works through it. The annoying recurring tickets that ate your old setup get fixed once instead of patched over and over.

What about your remote workers?

Standard offering now. Laptops with the right security, MFA on everything, hosted VoIP that follows people to their phones, remote-support sessions that work the same whether they're in your Orlando office, a hotel in Tampa, or a house in Winter Park. The help desk doesn't care where the user is.

How do you actually start?

Call. They run a discovery and assessment. They show you what they found, what they'd fix, and what it costs. You decide. If you go ahead, they get you fully onboarded over about 30 days and your staff starts calling the new number for everything IT-related.
